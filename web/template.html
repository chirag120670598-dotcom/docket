Docket

Three questions. Then every compliance deadline that actually binds your company — with the date, the penalty, and a link to the instrument itself. No login, no tracking, nothing leaves your browser.

Why a deadline list is worth nothing unless someone re-reads the law. The AI Act's high-risk obligations were due on 2 August 2026. They are now due on 2 December 2027, because the Digital Omnibus on AI — Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 — was published on 24 July 2026 and entered into force three days later. Any calendar built before that date is now confidently wrong, and the people hurt by it are the ones who planned around it.

That is the whole job. Not scraping dates, but re-reading the instrument and dating the moment it changed.

Who pays for this

Nobody, at the moment. There is no paid tier, no advertising, no tracking pixel, no email gate, and no investor to answer to. Hosting costs nothing and the code is open. The expensive part is the data — every one of the 23 regimes here has to be re-read against its own instrument, and a date that quietly moves is the one failure this page cannot survive.

How this works, and what it is not

Every entry names the instrument it comes from and the date it was last checked. primary means the legal text itself was read. reported means a secondary source was read and the instrument was not. Where a date has moved because of a later amendment, the amendment is named in the source line — the AI Act high-risk dates, for instance, are the ones set by the Digital Omnibus on AI, not the original Act.

This is a scoping aid, not legal advice. It tells you what to read and by when. It cannot tell you whether a particular product, activity or contract falls inside a definition — that is a legal question about facts only you have. Penalty figures are the statutory maxima, not predictions, and many regimes set penalties at national level after publication.

Coverage is deliberate rather than exhaustive: EU digital and product law, Indian labour and data law, and California packaging. A regime is added when the instrument can be read, not when a summary of it can be found. Missing a regime you need? Open an issue — that is how the list grows.
